TopTwo🗺️The level problem, and the rule that fixes it

You wrote“You already list AIMCo and BCI, which are investment managers, not single pension plans… otherwise you end up with the allocator equivalent of putting ‘Europe’ and ‘France’ in the same column.”
The rule this sheet now follows

A row earns a place in Section A if it can sign a subscription agreement for a fund it chose. Everything else is context. The Level column says which kind of thing each row is, and the “Decision sits with” column names the building to call.

TopSix🔎Three findings that change the call list

Suncor is the buried lead

Its 40-F restricts plan assets to “pooled, mutual, segregated or exchange traded funds”, and the private debt and equity sleeve went from 2% to 8% of C$7.76bn in a single year. No in-house private equity team, contractually confined to commingled vehicles, deploying now. That is the fund-of-funds buyer profile written out. The blocker is finding the person, not the fit.

Air Canada’s money is a real asset manager

Trans-Canada Capital runs the plans and third-party capital, and operates a C$9.0bn evergreen private-markets fund holding private equity and co-investments. Vincent Morin is President and CIO. The Pionairs hold no assets and are not investors.

Alberta is retrenching from Asia

AIMCo closed its Singapore and New York offices in February 2025, about 17 months after opening Singapore, and the head of Singapore left. Its 2025 Asia-Pacific activity is infrastructure, not private equity. A Singapore-based Asia fund-of-funds is walking into a retreat, not a gap. BCI is a harder version of the same problem: Asia is already 11.5% of its private equity book, directs are about 45% of it, and it sold C$1.6–1.9bn of secondaries in FY2025 to cut intermediated exposure.
